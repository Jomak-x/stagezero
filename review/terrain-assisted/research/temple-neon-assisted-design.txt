## Separate presentation mode

Implement **`KINEMATIC_TERRAIN_PRESENTATION`** as an offline postprocess. Preserve native motion verbatim outside this mode. Inside it, replace route translation, pelvis height, and leg articulation; retain native pelvis attitude and upper-body **local rotations**, transformed by route heading.

Assume z-up. Require a valid hip–knee–ankle mapping, bind-length skeleton, and calibrated sole-to-ankle transforms. **Bind offsets alone do not establish rendered sole height.** Reject materially inconsistent native positions/rotations rather than fitting them silently.

### 1. Plan contacts before solving poses

For one short, shallow flight:

- Extract approximate stance/swing intervals from native foot height and horizontal speed, with hysteresis. At 20 fps, treat these as timing proposals, not exact contacts; review or explicitly annotate ambiguous intervals.
- Preserve native left/right order and approximate cadence. Adjust route speed to place alternating contacts on valid treads—do not force arbitrary route progress onto fixed cadence.
- Place each landing near the centerline with native lateral stance width. Require the **whole sole footprint plus a margin** to lie on one horizontal tread. Never average heights across an edge.
- Store immutable world-space stance anchors, including sole orientation. Maintain them until toe-off; never re-query and reposition an already planted foot.
- For the minimal version, use flat, yaw-aligned planted feet. Ankle targets follow from the calibrated sole transform.

Plan the entire flight and its entry/exit contacts before playback.

### 2. Build collision-cleared swing targets

Between old and new anchors:

```text
xy(t) = smooth endpoint interpolation
z(t)  = smooth endpoint interpolation + planned clearance bump
```

Choose bump height and shape by sampling the **swept foot footprint**, not just the ankle:

```text
lowest_sole_z(t) >= highest_support_under_footprint(t) + clearance
```

Apply clearance away from intentional takeoff/landing contact. Raise the foot **before** crossing an upward edge; retain height until clear of a downward edge. Use near-zero endpoint velocity and blend native swing-foot rotation into flat landing orientation.

Interpolate positions and slerp rotations between native 20 fps samples; validate trajectories with adaptive subdivision around stair edges. Interpolation alone does not guarantee clearance.

### 3. Solve pelvis height from reachability—not native drift

Keep route-controlled pelvis horizontal position and native pelvis attitude. For leg \(i\):

- \(L_1,L_2\): bind segment lengths.
- \(a_i\): target ankle position.
- \(d_i\): hip offset from pelvis, transformed by pelvis attitude.
- \(\rho_i=\|p_{xy}+d_{i,xy}-a_{i,xy}\|\).
- Choose comfortable reach bounds \(D_{\min},D_{\max}\) from an allowed knee-flexion range; keep \(D_{\max}<L_1+L_2\).

Assuming the hip stays above the ankle:

\[
I_i=\left[
a_{i,z}-d_{i,z}+\sqrt{\max(0,D_{\min}^2-\rho_i^2)},
\quad
a_{i,z}-d_{i,z}+\sqrt{D_{\max}^2-\rho_i^2}
\right].
\]

If \(\rho_i>D_{\max}\), that target is infeasible.

Construct a preferred height:

```text
h_pref = planned support-elevation transition
       + calibrated comfortable pelvis height
       + bounded native vertical style residual
```

Use a small, zero-mean native residual—not accumulated native pelvis drift. Blend support elevation with anticipation through the transfer, rather than jumping to the newly contacted tread.

Offline, solve:

```text
minimize Σ (h - h_pref)² + λv(Δh)² + λa(Δ²h)²
subject to h ∈ intersection(I_left, I_right)
           configured vertical speed/acceleration limits
```

Include both stance and swing targets. **Do not independently smooth height afterward:** that can invalidate reachability. If infeasible, replan contact spacing, timing, or swing shape; never stretch bones or slide anchors.

### 4. Solve knees with a stable bend plane

For each leg:

```text
u = normalize(ankle - hip)
q = native knee displacement projected perpendicular to u
q = normalize(q), with previous-frame sign/continuity constraint
```

If the projection is weak, use the previous transported bend direction, then a configured anatomical forward-bend direction. Smooth that direction within the allowed knee plane; do not let it flip near extension.

With \(D=\|a-h\|\):

\[
x=\frac{L_1^2-L_2^2+D^2}{2D},\qquad
k=h+xu+\sqrt{L_1^2-x^2}\,q.
\]

Reconstruct thigh/shin rotations with consistent bind-axis conventions and restrained native twist. Apply the planned foot orientation. Keep upper-body local rotations unchanged.

### 5. Validate, then extend to repeated segments

Check between frames: sole clearance, anchor slip, knee limits, leg crossing, pelvis derivatives, and comfortable reach. Horizontal top-surface queries alone **cannot certify shin/body collisions, overhangs, or balance**; add geometry checks where those matter.

For repeated segments, carry forward absolute anchors, contact phase, knee-plane history, and pelvis boundary velocity. Plan overlapping horizons containing the next transfer; never reset height or phase independently at segment boundaries.

**Stop/reject** on ambiguous contact timing, insufficient tread footprint, infeasible reach/height constraints, excessive required motion correction, or unresolved collision. Use a separately planned reachable two-foot hold only if one exists. This is a kinematic stair presentation controller—not a dynamically balanced or universally natural locomotion system.
