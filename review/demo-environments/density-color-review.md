### Direction: concentrate activity, not clutter
The 44 m intersection likely feels empty because the visible space lacks a clear hierarchy. Aim for a **compact fictional Tokyo commercial junction**, not a miniature Shibuya. Build believable edges now; reserve pedestrian capacity for later.

- **Dimensions:** If 44 m means curb-to-curb, test reducing it to **26–30 m**, with **5–7 m sidewalks** and **8–10 m-deep corner gathering areas**. Use plausible **3–3.25 m traffic lanes**; avoid unexplained expanses of asphalt. Start with **4–5 m-wide perimeter crossings** and a **5–6 m diagonal crossing**. Keep their endpoints aligned with unobstructed pedestrian routes. These are visual blockout targets, not engineering specifications.
- **Urban enclosure:** Put **4–8-storey frontage** close to the sidewalk, with a few taller setbacks. Divide ground floors into **4–7 m storefront bays**. Recessed entrances, visible interiors and upper-floor windows provide useful scale; repeated blank walls make the junction feel larger.

### Palette and materials
Use **cool blue-gray asphalt**, slightly warmer concrete sidewalks and restrained off-white road paint. Anchor the scene with **warm amber storefront interiors**, cream façades and muted terracotta accents. Add selective cyan or desaturated green signage for contrast. Avoid uniform gray roughness and neon everywhere: subtle resurfacing patches, curb wear and material transitions will do more than scattered rubbish.

### Street furniture
Place signal poles near crossing approaches, outside the walking paths. Cluster lights, small utility cabinets and bicycle parking along the **outer sidewalk edge**, leaving a continuous **3–4 m clear route**. Put occasional planters or trees farther along the blocks, not across corner sightlines. Give each corner one recognizable landmark—an illuminated entrance, canopy or sign—rather than a pile of props.

### Camera
Test an elevated **three-quarter view, 6–9 m high, with a 35–45 mm full-frame-equivalent lens**. Frame two strong street walls and foreground curb detail; limit empty sky and distant asphalt. Also check pedestrian eye level: an attractive overview can hide implausible scale.
